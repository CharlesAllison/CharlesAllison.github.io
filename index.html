Molecular Biology · WPI Laboratory

Antimicrobial Resistance & Molecular Analysis

Isolated rifampicin-resistant E. coli and identified the precise genetic mutation responsible — a C1535A point mutation in the rpoB gene causing an S512Y amino acid substitution.

InstitutionWPI
OrganismE. coli
AntibioticRifampicin
Analysis ToolBenchling

Overview

This laboratory project investigated the molecular basis of antibiotic resistance in Escherichia coli. By combining microbiological selection with molecular biology techniques, I traced resistance down to a single nucleotide change in the RNA polymerase gene.

Methods

  • Isolated rifampicin-resistant colonies using aseptic technique, serial dilution, antibiotic selection plating, incubation, and colony counting
  • Amplified the rpoB hotspot region using PCR, optimizing conditions for clean amplification
  • Confirmed expected DNA fragment sizes via agarose gel electrophoresis
  • Purified PCR products for Sanger sequencing submission
  • Analyzed sequencing results in Benchling and identified mutation site

Key Finding

Sequence analysis revealed a C1535A point mutation in the rpoB gene, causing an S512Y (serine-to-tyrosine) amino acid substitution at a known hotspot associated with rifampicin resistance. This mutation disrupts the drug-binding site on RNA polymerase, preventing rifampicin from blocking transcription.

Molecular Biology · WPI Laboratory

DNA Purification & Restriction Mapping

Extracted, quantified, and characterized plasmid DNA from bacterial culture — confirming plasmid identity through restriction enzyme digestion and gel electrophoresis analysis.

Yield1.82 µg total
Concentration36.4 ng/µL
QuantificationNanoDrop
VerificationGel Electrophoresis

Overview

This experiment covered the complete workflow for plasmid DNA isolation and characterization — from bacterial lysis through restriction mapping — building proficiency in core molecular biology techniques.

Methods

  • Grew bacterial culture and harvested cells for plasmid extraction
  • Performed alkaline lysis to disrupt cells and denature chromosomal DNA
  • Purified plasmid DNA using silica spin column purification
  • Quantified DNA concentration and purity using NanoDrop spectrophotometry (260/280 ratio)
  • Performed restriction enzyme digestion to generate a characteristic fragment pattern
  • Resolved fragments by agarose gel electrophoresis and compared to expected sizes for plasmid identity confirmation

Results

Successfully purified plasmid DNA at 36.4 ng/µL with a total yield of 1.82 µg. Restriction digest fragment pattern matched the expected profile, confirming correct plasmid identity.
